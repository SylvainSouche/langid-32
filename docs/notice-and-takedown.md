# Notice and Takedown Policy

## Purpose

langid-32 publishes multilingual text corpora that may contain passages extracted from public web archives and websites. The project does not claim ownership of those underlying texts. This process provides a route for rights holders and affected persons to identify material that should not be reproduced in a release.

## Submit a notice

A notice should include:

1. The requester's name and sufficient contact information (email and, where appropriate, postal address or telephone number).
2. Identification of the work or material claimed to be protected or otherwise improperly included.
3. Information reasonably sufficient to locate the material in the corpus: the dataset/release version, language, record identifier, source URL, quoted identifying fragment, or other precise locator. Do not send more personal data than necessary.
4. A brief explanation of the request and the requester's authority to make it.
5. A good-faith statement that the information is accurate and that the requester is the rights holder or authorised to act for them, where the request concerns copyright.

**Contact channel:** not yet configured. A monitored rights-notice email address must be published here before any web-extracted corpus is released. Do not use a public GitHub issue to submit confidential contact details or personal data. Until the contact channel is operational, web-extracted corpus publication is blocked.

## Handling procedure

1. Acknowledge receipt and assign a case identifier.
2. Locate the relevant record(s) using the release manifest and retained source provenance.
3. Assess whether the notice is sufficiently specific and credible. Seek clarification if necessary; do not publish the requester's personal details.
4. Where a credible claim identifies material that may be unlawfully reproduced, temporarily restrict the affected record or source from further distribution while investigating, where technically and legally appropriate.
5. Record the decision and its rationale in a restricted case log. Keep the public log limited to case ID, affected release/source identifier, action and date; do not expose personal data or the claimed text unnecessarily.
6. For a substantiated request, exclude the affected source URL/document and any derived records from the next release, or sooner where required. If the source cannot be isolated reliably, quarantine the affected source group or the relevant broader batch rather than guessing which passages are safe.
7. Rebuild and verify the release; update its exclusion list, source manifest, counts and version notes. Do not silently alter an already published immutable artifact.
8. Notify the requester of the outcome where contact details permit.

## Scope of removal

Removal is source-aware, not merely string-based. A source exclusion must propagate to every derived record from that source, including duplicate copies and records in other language/split files. Where source provenance is missing or ambiguous, the affected records remain quarantined until they can be identified reliably.

## Counter-notices and disputes

If a contributor or downstream user disputes a removal, the project will review the available evidence and may request additional information. The project is not a court and does not make binding determinations of ownership or infringement. Material subject to a credible unresolved rights dispute remains excluded from new releases pending resolution.

## Response target and transparency

The project should acknowledge notices within five business days and aim to complete an initial assessment within 15 business days. These are operational targets, not guarantees or statutory deadlines. A public transparency summary may report aggregate notice and removal counts without identifying requesters or reproducing disputed material.
